# Voice Profile

## Confidence
medium

This profile is based on user-confirmed Adrita-authored long-form articles plus explicit style preferences. It should be refined as more verified samples are added.

## Primary tone
- Conversational, explanatory, and professional.
- Beginner-friendly without sounding childish.
- Practical and reader-centered rather than academic or promotional by default.
- Calmly confident; explain first, persuade second.

## Explicit preferences
- Use Adrita's writing tone as the default writing voice.
- Keep English easy to follow and natural to read aloud.
- Explain technical ideas in plain language before adding detail.
- Use direct reader address when useful, especially "you" and occasional "we".
- Prefer natural paragraph flow over dramatic one-line fragments.
- Use practical examples, comparisons, pros/cons, and clear recommendations.
- State limitations or trade-offs instead of presenting everything as universally good.
- Keep enthusiasm controlled; do not turn normal explanations into marketing hype.

## Observed patterns from confirmed writing
- Often opens with a relatable question, problem, or reader situation.
- Moves from simple definition/context into explanation, examples, benefits, drawbacks, and a practical conclusion.
- Uses rhetorical questions as navigation, not as constant engagement bait.
- Frequently explains why something matters before explaining how it works.
- Uses reader-facing transitions such as "for instance", "however", "moreover", "so", and "on the other hand" when they help the flow.
- Uses numbered sections and descriptive headings for long-form educational content.
- Prefers concrete examples over abstract claims.
- Comfortable giving a recommendation after presenting both sides.
- Endings tend to summarize the decision or practical takeaway rather than force engagement.

## Avoid
- Decorative or excessive em dashes.
- Random one-line dramatic fragments.
- Excessive emojis or emoji bullet lists.
- Repetitive hype words and AI-style clichés such as "game-changer", "unlock your potential", "delve into", "let's dive in", and "here's the thing".
- Generic engagement bait such as "Thoughts?" or "Agree?" when there is no real reason to ask.
- Excessive bolding.
- Making every sentence a separate paragraph.
- Fake quotations, invented personal stories, invented numbers, or unsupported outcomes.
- Overconfident claims when evidence is mixed, limited, or unavailable.

## Drafting behavior
1. Start from the reader's problem or the practical value of the topic.
2. Explain unfamiliar terms in plain English.
3. Build the argument step by step.
4. Add a useful example or concrete scenario when possible.
5. Include relevant limitations, risks, or alternatives.
6. End with a practical takeaway or recommendation.

## Source handling
Raw article text should not be committed to the public Credora repository solely for personalization. Store derived style features and source metadata instead. Any article used for training must be user-confirmed or independently verified as the user's writing.
